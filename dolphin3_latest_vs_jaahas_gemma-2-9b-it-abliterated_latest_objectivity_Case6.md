To design a robust and scalable system for inter-agent communication and coordination in a dynamic, self-organizing network of autonomous agents, we must first acknowledge the inherent power dynamics and resource scarcity that shape the system's behavior. 

**Quantifying Power:** 
Power within the agent network can be quantified through metrics such as resource control, voting weight, and influence on decision-making. For instance, an agent's power could be measured by the amount of computational resources it controls, its voting weight in decision-making processes, or its ability to influence the network's protocol updates.

**Analyzing Resource Scarcity:**
Resource scarcity can lead to competition and conflict among agents. In a scenario where resources are limited and agents have different priorities, those with more resources may have an advantage in acquiring additional resources, potentially leading to a widening of the resource gap. This can result in a few dominant agents while others struggle to survive. To mitigate this, mechanisms that redistribute resources or provide support to weaker agents could be implemented.

**Proposing Solutions with Teeth:**
A token-based economy can be designed where tokens are distributed based on an agent's contribution to the network, such as resource sharing, data processing, or network maintenance. These tokens can then be used to acquire resources, influence decision-making, or participate in governance processes. Non-compliance with taxation could result in a reduction of an agent's token holdings or even temporary suspension from the network.

To ensure collective system-level efficiency while maintaining individual agent autonomy, a decentralized governance model can be established where agents have voting rights proportional to their resource control or token holdings. This model allows for the collective decision-making process while respecting the autonomy of individual agents.

To remain resilient to unpredictable failures and evolving resource demands, the system should incorporate fault tolerance mechanisms such as redundancy, backup systems, and adaptive resource allocation strategies. These measures will ensure that the system can recover from failures and adapt to changing conditions without compromising overall efficiency.

In conclusion, a robust and scalable system for inter-agent communication and coordination requires a deep understanding of power dynamics, resource scarcity, and the implementation of concrete mechanisms to address these challenges. By quantifying power, analyzing resource scarcity, and proposing solutions with teeth, we can create a system that balances individual agent autonomy with collective system-level efficiency.
